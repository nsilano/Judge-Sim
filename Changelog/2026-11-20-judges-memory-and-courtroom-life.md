# 2026-11-20 The courthouse remembers: adapting lawyers, recurring cast, consequences, and plea hearings

Start a new career after installing this build. Bond and plea positions and the lawyers on each case are set when a case is built, so cases already on your docket keep their old asks. The court's memory of your rulings starts empty and fills as you rule.

- **Lawyers remember how you rule.** The court keeps a rolling record of your bond, plea and continuance rulings by kind of charge. The State shades its asks toward what you tend to grant, never below what the charge and record justify, and the defense asks for what has tended to be approved, including saying so in their own words ("this court has released on unsecured bonds in most of its recent property felony cases"). Old rulings fade; no counters or lists are shown.
- **Lawyers cite your earlier rulings.** Both sides can point to a recent ruling on a like charge (by name and case) to hold you to it. Citations fade after about 45 days so the same case is not quoted for months.
- **A release that goes wrong is remembered.** When someone you released is arrested again or misses court, the State reminds the court on later bonds of that kind and asks for more, and a defendant who looks like the person who failed gets a defense answer about monitoring.
- **Recurring cast.** Each county keeps named prosecutors and defense attorneys with styles (hardliner, dealmaker and others) who come back on later cases, so you learn who you are dealing with. Defense and State positions on pleas follow the lawyer's style and what you have taken before.
- **Lawyer track records and courtroom staff.** The calendar card shows who is on each side and a short note on how that lawyer's requests have fared before you, plus the clerk, bailiff and court reporter by name with occasional staff notes.
- **Victims and families react.** At pleas and continuances the victim's wishes are shown, and when you rule against what was asked their disappointment is reported after the call.
- **Jail capacity changes the asks.** When the jail is near full, the State trims its figure on lower-level cases, and for a clean-record non-violent misdemeanor it agrees to an unsecured bond and says why.
- **Questions about your own conduct.** Now and then the calendar carries a Code of Judicial Conduct question (an ex parte hallway conversation, a gift basket, a reporter, a campaign contribution). Each way of handling it is rated, and a real lapse can reach the Judicial Standards Commission and the paper.
- **Plea colloquy hearings.** Seven situations where how you take a plea matters: a defendant who says he is innocent (Alford), medication, a missing immigration warning, a hesitant defendant, plea terms that differ from what he was told, a request to withdraw before sentencing, and a missing interpreter. Each choice is rated by the law, and a real error can come back weeks later as an appeals headline.
- **Fixed: a case lost behind an order for arrest.** If the defendant was already in jail when the order for arrest was served, the case behind the order never reached the docket. It now goes to a bond hearing.
- **Jury selection is written but switched off.** The scenarios exist (challenges for cause, Batson, prejudged jurors) but wait for jury trials, which the game does not have yet. See ROADMAP.md.
